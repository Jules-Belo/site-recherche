In competitive bouldering, performance depends on the ability to generate movement solutions that are both novel and functionally effective, a capacity defined as motor creativity. Within the ecological dynamics framework, movement variability constitutes a functional resource that enables athletes to adapt to task and environmental constraints. However, no study has examined how muscular fatigue constrains motor creativity in boulder climbing. This study aims to explore peripheral fatigue's impact on motor creativity and the moderating role of regulatory focus orientation on these variables. Twenty-eight climbers classified according to their regulatory focus orientation, assessed using the QORS (14 promotion focus and 14 prevention focus), were tasked with climbing two boulder problems, one under non-fatigued conditions and one under fatigued conditions, counterbalanced across participants. 
Each boulder problem offered at least three distinct and valid solutions. Participants had ten minutes per boulder, with the first goal to top the boulder, and the second goal was to find as many different ways as possible to complete the boulder. Then, fatigue was induced with the SmartBoard (force measurement device) using forty-five seconds of maximal dynamic pull-ups and twenty-four isometric finger flexor contractions (ten seconds of effort at 80% of MVC and six seconds of rest between contractions).
Participants then completed the second boulder problem under the same instructions. 2D hip position data were analyzed through symmetrized segment-path distance (SSPD) to determine the number of distinct successful trajectories performed (variability) and the degree of originality of each successful trajectory (creativity). 

Preliminary results indicated that movement creativity and variability results tended to decrease after fatigue, and most experienced climbers appeared to achieve higher creativity scores. The only result that is clearly relevant to date is fatigue induction with a mean force decrease of 40.2% ± 8.9% (n = 16).



# Revu

Regulatory Focus (RF) distinguishes two motivational orientations: promotion (seeking gains) and prevention (avoiding losses). In sport, RF may influence creativity – the ability to make original yet effective decisions. In competitive bouldering, performance depends on the ability to generate movement solutions that are both novel and functionally effective (motor creativity). Within the ecological dynamics framework, movement variability constitutes a functional resource that enables athletes to adapt to task and environment constraints. To date, no study has examined how muscular fatigue constrains motor creativity in boulder climbing. This study explored peripheral fatigue's impact on motor creativity considering  climbers’ regulatory focus profiles. Twenty-eight climbers climbed two boulder problems (counterbalanced, one under no-fatigue and one under-fatigue). Boulders offered at least three distinct, valid solutions. Participants had to top the boulder, and find as many options as possible to do so. Fatigue was induced with a SmartBoard using 45secs of maximal dynamic pull-ups and 24 isometric finger flexor contractions (with 10secs of effort at 80% of MVC and 6secs of rest between contractions). Two-dimensional hip position data were analysed through symmetrised segment-path distance to determine the number of distinct successful trajectories performed (variability) and the degree of originality of each successful trajectory (creativity). Preliminary results indicated that creativity and variability tended to decrease with fatigue and that most experienced climbers produced higher creativity performances. Further analysis considering RF profiles and the association with climbing performance and both creativity and variability are ongoing and will be discussed considering existing literature.
